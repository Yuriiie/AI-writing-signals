# AI-writing-signals

A writing analysis skill that identifies common patterns associated with AI-generated text, based on the [WikiProject AI Cleanup's "Signs of AI writing" catalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).

Rather than attempting to determine whether a text was written by AI or a human, this skill highlights stylistic patterns, evaluates their significance, and helps users make informed editorial decisions.

## Overview

AI-generated writing often exhibits recurring patterns, such as exaggerated conclusions, vague attributions, promotional language, repetitive structures, and unnecessary explanations.

This skill helps identify these patterns through contextual analysis, distinguishing meaningful indicators from ordinary writing habits.

It can also revise text to improve clarity, naturalness, and authenticity without sacrificing the author's original meaning or voice.

## Features

- **Pattern identification** — Detects stylistic patterns associated with AI-generated writing.
- **Contextual evaluation** — Considers genre, purpose, and surrounding context before evaluating a signal.
- **Evidence-based analysis** — Quotes the exact passages that triggered an observation.
- **Signal weighting** — Distinguishes weak indicators from stronger and more meaningful patterns.
- **Human-oriented reporting** — Explains findings without assigning arbitrary AI probability scores.
- **Text rewriting** — Revises flagged passages while preserving the author's voice and intended meaning.
- **Substantive review** — Highlights vague claims, unsupported statistics, questionable citations, and other underlying issues.

## How It Works

The skill follows a structured review process:

1. Reads the complete reference catalog before analyzing the text.
2. Reads the entire text to understand its genre, purpose, and context.
3. Identifies relevant writing patterns and quotes the exact occurrences.
4. Evaluates each signal according to its strength and context.
5. Considers multiple signals together rather than treating isolated patterns as proof.
6. Checks verifiable claims, citations, links, and statistics when possible.
7. Presents the findings and suggests improvements when appropriate.

## Analysis Principles

### No definitive authorship claims

No writing pattern proves that a text was generated by AI.

Human writers naturally use structures and expressions that can also appear in AI-generated writing. Therefore, the skill never labels a text definitively as "AI-written" or "human-written."

### Context matters

A promotional tone in marketing copy is expected. The same tone in an encyclopedia article may be suspicious.

Patterns are evaluated according to the text's purpose, genre, and context.

### Multiple signals matter more than isolated ones

A single weak indicator should not trigger an alarm. Several patterns from different categories appearing together may justify closer examination.

### Substance comes before style

Removing stylistic patterns is not enough. Unsupported claims, fabricated citations, misleading statistics, and empty analysis are more important problems than superficial wording.

## Usage

Use this skill whenever you need to:

- Review a text for signs of AI-generated writing.
- Check whether a draft sounds artificial or overly generic.
- Audit text generated by AI tools before publishing.
- Identify common AI writing habits.
- Rewrite text to sound more natural and authentic.
- Improve a draft without losing the author's voice.
- Investigate questionable citations, vague claims, or unsupported information.

### Example requests

> Analyze this text for signs of AI writing.

> Does this paragraph sound artificial?

> Review this article using the AI writing patterns catalog.

> Rewrite this text to sound more natural, but preserve my writing style.

> Check this Claude-generated draft before I publish it.

## Reporting Format

When analyzing text, the skill provides:

1. **Overall assessment** — A concise summary of the relevant signals and their combined strength.
2. **Detected patterns** — Only the categories actually found.
3. **Evidence** — Exact excerpts from the original text.
4. **Context** — An explanation of why each signal matters, or why it may be insignificant.
5. **Substantive concerns** — Claims, sources, statistics, or arguments that require further verification.

It avoids unnecessary lists of patterns that were not found.

## Rewriting

When asked to rewrite a text, the skill prioritizes:

- Replacing unsupported praise with concrete information.
- Removing unnecessary conclusions and repetitive explanations.
- Rewriting unnatural contrast structures into direct statements.
- Removing decorative formatting when it does not serve the destination.
- Preserving the original meaning and the author's voice.
- Avoiding mechanical synonym replacement.
- Flagging claims that cannot be verified.

The goal is not to eliminate every possible AI writing signal. The goal is to produce clearer, more natural, and more meaningful writing.

## Limitations

- Writing patterns cannot reliably determine authorship.
- False positives are possible.
- The catalog was developed around observations of AI-generated text, particularly in English.
- Patterns must be adapted to other languages rather than translated mechanically.
- AI detection tools are not used or replaced by this skill.
- A stylistically natural text may still contain factual errors.
- A text containing AI-associated patterns may have been written entirely by a human.

This skill is an editorial aid, not an authorship verification system.

## Philosophy

**Patterns are evidence for closer examination, not proof of authorship.**

The purpose is to help people critically evaluate writing, improve its quality, and identify problems that matter — not to accuse writers or mechanically sanitize text.
